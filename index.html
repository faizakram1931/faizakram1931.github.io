My work, layer by layer

01Hardware & optics

Near-field optics that let a phone camera read a strip less than a centimetre away.

  • Prism, mirror and glass optics tuned for sharpness, focus and contrast.
  • Backlight design, with LEDs binned against datasheet behaviour and CIELAB targets.

02Firmware

Illumination control, plus a data link from device to phone through light.

  • Arduino/C++ backlight timing and PWM, balancing image quality against battery life.
  • A four-LED blink pattern that sends device data to the phone through its camera.

03Phone software

A camera platform across 75+ Android models and every iPhone from the 6 on.

  • Real-time preview processing at ~20 FPS; iOS (Swift, Objective-C, C++) and Android (Java, NDK).
  • Front-camera device-attach detection with no extra electronics.
  • On-device AI that ran ~6 years across new iPhones without retraining.

04Quantification

Turning line colour into hormone concentration, normalised for every phone.

  • On-device calibration chart used to estimate a per-phone colour-correction matrix.
  • Per-user baseline, rise and surge detection instead of fixed thresholds.

05Assay & biology

Reading built around lateral-flow assay behaviour for LH and E3G.

  • Co-authored the IEEE Sensors Journal paper on smartphone LFA quantification of LH and E3G (2020).
  • Hormone time-series interpretation, later explored with attention-based 1D models.

06Manufacturing

Machine-vision QC, strip identity and traceability for every part shipped.

  • Conveyor strip qualification at ~200 ms per strip.
  • Encrypted Aztec code scheme addressing ~2B strips without collisions.
  • Optics defect inspection and print-drift checks on calibration charts.

02 · Systems

Product 1

Edge AI that outlasted the hardware

Replaced a brittle multi-scale template matcher with a quantized on-device classifier that kept working across new iPhone generations. Edge models from this work run on 200K+ phones in the US.

The problem

The template matcher worked on the phones it was tuned for and broke on new camera hardware, making every new phone release a support risk.

What I did

  • Trained a deep-learning classifier to replace the classical detector.
  • Quantized it for on-device inference and integrated it into the production iOS app.
  • Followed with further edge models, owning training, optimisation, integration and production behaviour.

Outcome

Near-100% performance with no observed failures after launch, and roughly six years across newer iPhones without retraining.

~200 msper strip inspected
Product 1

Manufacturing machine vision

Real-time QC built from scratch: optical defect inspection, print-drift checks, LED colour binning and strip qualification, linked by traceability.

  • Optical components imaged for dust, fibres, burrs and scratches, each linked to a QR code.
  • Calibration charts checked for colour drift straight after printing.
  • LEDs binned against datasheet behaviour and target CIELAB regions.
  • Strips qualified on a conveyor with machine cameras in about 200 ms each.

A part's history follows it from inspection through assembly to the field, rather than stopping at a pass/fail gate.

~92%agreement with the dedicated reader
Product 2

Diagnostics from a phone photo

A cascaded classification, detection, segmentation and regression pipeline that showed computation could replace purpose-built optical hardware.

  • Classification rejects poor-quality, occluded or contaminated photos.
  • Detection localises the features the measurement depends on.
  • Segmentation extracts precise regions of interest.
  • Regression estimates the final quantitative value.

I owned the whole path from phone capture to backend result: data flow, model stages, deployment and integration.

Product 2

Lateral-flow assays in silico

Mechanistic models of flow, transport and binding kinetics so strip designs can be screened before wet-lab work, paired with Monte Carlo simulation of the full ten-track product.

  • Capillary flow and time-to-reach along the strip.
  • Association/dissociation kinetics between antibody, analyte, conjugate and capture.
  • Any number of test and control lines, with signal accumulation mapped to readout.
  • Bootstrap and correlation-aware replica models built from measured signal distributions.

In production

AI-designed antibodies that reach the strip

Protein folding, design and docking on a multi-GPU orchestration layer I built from scratch. Antibodies designed with this platform now go into strips in production, so the applied-AI loop closes in a manufactured product, not just a ranked candidate list.

Programs

Spanning testosterone and cortisol antibodies, insulin binders, and antibodies for the strip assays themselves.

How it works

  • Structure prediction with AlphaFold-family tools and ImmuneBuilder.
  • Antibody and binder design with RFdiffusion-style generation and ProteinMPNN.
  • Docking and affinity with AutoDock tooling, OpenMM, AmberTools and MMPBSA.
  • Candidates ranked against CDR definitions, docking energy and patent-coverage rules before wet-lab follow-up.
  • A Flask platform that puts folding, docking and batch runs in front of the wider team.

03 · Approach

How I choose tools

Each shift in technique came from a specific bottleneck, not a trend.

Deep learning, when generalisation broke

Template matching couldn't keep up with new phone cameras, so a learned model replaced it.

Computation instead of hardware

The photo-only pipeline tested whether AI could absorb what a dedicated optical reader did.

Simulation, when wet-lab cycles were the cost

Every assay iteration takes reagents, fabrication and imaging, so the simulator narrows the search first.

Monte Carlo, when averages hid the risk

Product-level uncertainty only shows up when variability is pushed through the full decision logic.

GPU orchestration, when one machine wasn't enough

Modern protein design needed many GPUs running in parallel.

Retrieval, when knowledge walked out the door

Support answers lived in people's heads, so past tickets became searchable context.
