Selected work

~6 yrsin production without retraining

Edge AI that outlasted the hardware

Replaced a brittle multi-scale template matcher with a quantized on-device classifier that kept working across new iPhone generations. Edge models from this work now run on 200K+ phones in the US.

The problem

The original detector used multi-scale template matching. It worked on the phones it was tuned for and broke on new camera hardware, which made every new phone release a support risk.

What I did

  • Trained a deep-learning classifier to replace the classical detector.
  • Quantized it for on-device inference and integrated it into the production iOS app.
  • Followed up with further edge detection and classification models, owning training, optimisation, mobile integration and production behaviour.

Outcome

Near-100% performance with no observed failures in the months after launch, and roughly six years across newer iPhones without retraining, based on production observation.

~92%agreement with the dedicated reader

Diagnostics from a phone photo

A cascaded classification, detection, segmentation and regression pipeline that reads test strips from smartphone photos, testing whether computation can replace purpose-built optical hardware.

How the cascade works

  • Classification rejects photos with poor quality, occlusion or foreign objects.
  • Detection localises the features the measurement depends on.
  • Segmentation extracts precise regions of interest.
  • Regression estimates the final quantitative value.

Scope

I owned the whole path from phone capture to backend result, not a single model: data flow, model stages, deployment and integration. In comparative validation the result agreed with the dedicated reader about 92% of the time.

~200 msper strip inspected

Manufacturing machine vision

Real-time QC built from scratch: optical defect inspection, print-drift checks, LED colour binning and strip qualification, linked by traceability. Associated systems have qualified ~30M strips and 300K+ devices.

What gets inspected

  • Optical components, imaged with high-definition machine cameras for dust, fibres, burrs and scratches, each part linked to a QR code.
  • Calibration charts, checked for colour drift against reference standards straight after printing so printer drift is caught before assembly.
  • LEDs, binned against datasheet behaviour and target CIELAB colour regions.
  • Strips, qualified on a conveyor with machine cameras in about 200 ms each.

The point was a measurable production system, where a part's history follows it from inspection through assembly to the field, rather than a pass/fail gate.

N linesconfigurable assay simulator

Lateral-flow assays in silico

Mechanistic models of capillary flow, transport and binding kinetics for sandwich and competitive assays, so designs can be screened before wet-lab work. Paired with Monte Carlo simulation of the full multi-track product.

What the simulator models

  • Capillary flow and time-to-reach along the strip.
  • Analyte and probe transport, and association/dissociation kinetics between antibody, analyte, conjugate and capture.
  • Strip dimensions and line geometry, with any number of test and control lines.
  • Signal accumulation over time and its mapping to a measurable readout.

Product-level Monte Carlo

For the ten-track product I simulate the whole strip, not just a model. Assay variability is propagated through the multi-track decision logic to estimate system accuracy on virtual datasets far larger than physical testing allows, using bootstrap and correlation-aware replica models built from measured signal distributions.

20–30GPUs orchestrated

Antibody design at GPU scale

Protein folding, design and docking workflows on a multi-GPU orchestration layer I built from scratch, generating ranked computational candidates for an IP-oriented testosterone antibody program.

Workflows

  • Structure prediction and evaluation with AlphaFold-family tools and ImmuneBuilder.
  • Binder design with RFdiffusion-style generation and ProteinMPNN sequence design, including work on insulin binders.
  • Docking and affinity estimation with AutoDock-related tooling, OpenMM, AmberTools and MMPBSA.
  • Mutation and review tooling that accounts for CDR definitions, flexible docking residues, docking energies and patent-coverage rules.

Infrastructure

An orchestration layer that distributes independent jobs across 20–30 GPUs at once and merges results, reusable for new targets. A Flask web platform puts sequence comparison, generation, folding, docking and CSV-driven batch runs in front of the wider team, with concurrency limits matched to capacity.

Next-generation diagnostic platform

Strip geometry, on-device capture engine, cloud ML on Docker and Vertex AI, multi-track consensus logic, drift and defect analytics, and verification tooling. In development for clinical studies and an intended 510(k) submission.

Designing the strip for the camera

From the product-design stage: strip size, track width and spacing, background contrast, QR size and placement, and embossed features to reduce shadowing, each turned from imaging studies into explicit requirements.

Capture and backend

  • A capture engine that checks geometry and image quality on live preview frames, guides the user and keeps the best analytical frame.
  • One camera library shared by production, data collection, model training and verification, so models never see a different acquisition path from the one they'll run on.
  • The frontend-to-backend contract, the image-processing pipeline, the set of models needed, and containerised Flask services on Vertex AI.

Making ten tracks agree

Intermediate cutoffs use several replica tracks and a consensus decision rather than trusting one track. I evaluate per-track behaviour, false-positive and false-negative rates and replica tolerance.

Manufacturing and verification

  • A two-stage conjugate binning workflow where bin definitions are locked as controlled files, so operators can't retune processing during classification.
  • Scanner-to-scanner drift analysis with comparison dashboards.
  • Simulated dust, bubbles and foreign objects over the analytical regions to find the smallest defect that changes the result.
  • A verification jig with about five degrees of freedom to test capture robustness under realistic pose changes.
RAGfor customer support

Keeping institutional knowledge

Historical resolved tickets turned into a retrieval index that drafts replies for support agents, so answers survive when people move on.

  • Resolved tickets and agent replies processed into retrieval-ready knowledge units.
  • Embeddings stored in a vector database; new tickets retrieve the most similar past cases.
  • Retrieved context goes to an LLM that drafts a reply for an agent to review, cutting drafting time per ticket.

Earlier foundations

Before the systems above, the first-generation product needed a lot of smaller problems solved, mostly from scratch.

Near-field optics

Prism, mirror and glass optics that let a phone camera image a target less than about a centimetre away, tuned for sharpness, focus and contrast.

Firmware and talking through light

Arduino/C++ firmware for backlight timing and PWM to balance imaging against battery life, plus a four-LED blink pattern that sent device data to the phone through its camera.

Device sensing with the front camera

A preview-frame classifier and state machine that detected when the device was attached or removed, without any extra electronics.

A camera platform for 100+ phones

Camera selection and control across 75+ Android models and every iPhone from about the 6 onward, processing preview frames at around 20 FPS for real-time feedback.

Colour calibration

An on-device calibration chart used to estimate a colour-correction matrix, normalising each phone's colour response for quantitative imaging.

Strip identity

An encrypted code scheme addressing about 2 billion strips without collisions, printed as Aztec codes and decoded from phone images to prevent reuse and support traceability.

Hormone time series

Algorithms that find baselines, rises and surges in each user's own history rather than fixed thresholds, later explored with attention-based 1D models.

How I choose tools

Each shift in technique came from a specific bottleneck, not a trend.

Deep learning, when generalisation broke

Template matching couldn't keep up with new phone cameras, so a learned model replaced it.

Computation instead of hardware

The photo-only pipeline tested whether AI could absorb what a dedicated optical reader did.

Simulation, when wet-lab cycles were the cost

Every assay iteration takes reagents, fabrication and imaging, so the simulator narrows the search first.

Monte Carlo, when averages hid the risk

Product-level uncertainty only shows up when variability is pushed through the full decision logic.

GPU orchestration, when one machine wasn't enough

Modern protein design needed many GPUs running in parallel.

Retrieval, when knowledge walked out the door

Support answers lived in people's heads, so past tickets became searchable context.
